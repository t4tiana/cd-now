# Historical market context for the CDnow assessment

Assume an investment memo dated **July 15, 1998**. Use external company and market information only when its original publication date is on or before that date; an archive's upload date is not its original publication date. General methodology literature may be newer for learning and method justification, without supplying later facts or outcomes about CDnow. These sources give context, not an explanation established by the transaction file. The [source audit](historical_source_audit.md) records each article's provenance, supporting passage, and any conflict. The [company evidence inventory](company_financial_evidence_1998.md) records contemporaneous financial reporting, SEC disclosures, and remaining gaps.

- The Recording Industry Association of America's 1997 year-end figures, released in February 1998, showed a **6.5% decline in total domestic audio and video unit shipments** but a **2.3% increase in full-length CD unit shipments to retail**. The measures are manufacturer shipments, not CDnow sales or customer-level demand. The mixed picture does not establish a broad CD retail collapse that explains the Q1 1997 cohort's later activity. [Contemporaneous report of the RIAA release](https://www.stereophile.com/content/riaa-releases-1997-year-end-music-sales-statistics).
- February 1998 reporting described CDnow as operating since **1994** and identified growing competition in online music retail, including Music Boulevard and distribution partnerships. This rules out a 1997 company launch as the reason every customer appears in Q1 1997, but competition is only a **possible** contributor to later behavior, not a cause identified by this dataset. [Joe Ashbrook Nickell, *Wired*, February 11](https://www.wired.com/1998/02/cdnow-goes-to-market/).
- [Barry Willis's May 3 report](https://www.stereophile.com/content/cdnow-tops-chart-expands-europe) relayed CDnow's **$10 million of company-wide Q1 1998 revenue** and **135,000 added registered customers since January 1**, bringing its cumulative registered count to **432,000**. These are company-reported figures, not measurements of this supplied cohort; the article does not establish that every registered customer purchased. [Gregg Wirth's May 15 report](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/stocks/cdnows-planned-stock-offering-may-hit-a-sour-note-with-investors-15175) independently reported the $10 million Q1 1998 revenue, **$2.6 million Q1 1997 revenue**, and a **$9.2 million Q1 1998 net loss**. It reported the earlier **1997 full-year net loss as $10.7 million**, consistent with [February *Wired* reporting](https://www.wired.com/1998/02/cdnow-goes-to-market/). The May 3 article instead attaches $9.2 million to “last year”; its period conflicts with the other two sources. Company-wide sales growth does not prove this cohort's economics, and the loss does not reveal its contribution or repayment capacity.
- A [March 21 *Billboard* report](https://books.google.com/books?id=2Q4EAAAAMBAJ&pg=PA113) cited CDnow's SEC documents for roughly **15% company-wide 1997 gross margin** and **$9.2 million marketing costs**. I checked the supplied [February prospectus](../CDNOW_data/SEC_424B1.txt) directly: its exact 1997 figures are **16.3% gross margin** (gross profit divided by net sales) and **$9.24 million sales and marketing**. Neither is the margin or acquisition cost of the proposed financed cohort. [May 15 reporting](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/stocks/cdnows-planned-stock-offering-may-hit-a-sour-note-with-investors-15175) relayed **$60.5 million of IPO proceeds still on hand at March 31**; a [June 3 report](https://www.wired.com/1998/06/net-ad-firms-join-forces/) said CDnow called off a proposed further stock sale. The March cash balance does not establish cash on July 15.

## Online music growth and a future cohort

These contemporaneous market estimates concern **sales dollars across sellers**, whereas the proposed cohort is defined by **CDnow first purchasers in a quarter**. I use them to test plausible cohort-size scenarios, not as a multiplier for first purchasers. The underlying Jupiter Communications reports were unavailable, so the exact coverage and methods cannot be reconciled.

| Claim and measure | Source and passage checked | Limit for this decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jupiter estimated **$18.3 million of 1996 online CD sales across about a dozen sites** and forecast **$110 million in 1998**. | *Wired*, Mike Romano, “Amazon.com Moves toward Music, Video Sales,” July 22, 1997, [article](https://www.wired.com/1997/07/amazon-com-moves-toward-music-video-sales/), paragraphs beginning “The online music industry” and “Tower Online.” | The $110 million was a pre-cutoff forecast, not measured 1998 sales. Sales can change through buyer count, order frequency, order size, price, or market share. |
| Jupiter estimated **$49 million of 1997 online music-retailing sales**. | *Wired*, Jennifer Sullivan, “Custom Discs Give CDnow New Spin,” June 3, 1998, [article](https://www.wired.com/1998/06/custom-discs-give-cdnow-new-spin/), paragraph beginning “Many figure.” | The implied increase from $18.3 million would be about 168% **if the definitions match**. The articles do not establish that they do or that CDnow first purchasers rose at this rate. |
| CDnow reported approximately **209,000 first purchasers during all of 1997**; the supplied Q1 cohort contains **23,570**. | CDnow, Inc., [February 9, 1998 prospectus](../CDNOW_data/SEC_424B1.txt), pp. 3, 5–6, filed February 11; dataset [readme](../CDNOW_data/read_me_CDNOW_master.txt). | If definitions align, about 185,430 first purchased in Q2–Q4 combined, or 61,810 per quarter on average. No individual later-quarter count is disclosed. |
| CDnow's Q1 1998 company sales were about **$10 million**, and it reported **135,000 new registrations**. | *Stereophile*, Barry Willis, “CDnow Tops Chart, Expands into Europe,” May 3, 1998, [article](https://www.stereophile.com/content/cdnow-tops-chart-expands-europe), paragraphs beginning “The numbers are up” and “CDnow has registered.” | Sales include repeat customers. Registrations are not first purchases. Neither is a Q4 1998 first-purchaser count. |
| Amazon announced a music store on **June 11, 1998**. | Amazon.com, Inc., [press release](https://press.aboutamazon.com/1998/6/amazon-com-opens-music-store-provides-a-whole-new-way-to-discover-music), opening announcement. | Market growth does not ensure CDnow keeps its share or acquires customers at the same cost. This is a contemporaneous competitive fact, not a forecast of later outcomes. |

The $49 million 1997 estimate and $110 million 1998 forecast suggest a larger market if their definitions match, but do not justify doubling CDnow's Q4 new-purchaser count. Market demand, CDnow's share, partner traffic conversion to first purchases, and incremental marketing spend remain separate assumptions. A [February *Wired* article](https://www.wired.com/1998/02/cdnow-goes-to-market/) reported CDnow's 1997 share at about **15%**, while Sullivan's June article reported about **33%**; both attributed their estimates to Jupiter. I do not use either share because their underlying definitions and methods have not been reconciled.

## How to pair the sources with cohort findings

| Observed cohort question | Contemporary context | Limit on the inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why is Q1 1997 larger than later quarters? | CDnow had operated since 1994. | Every supplied customer had to make their first recorded purchase in Q1 1997 by definition. The cohort file cannot establish what caused their entry. |
| Does falling activity in this cohort mean CDnow itself was shrinking? | Third-party reports relayed higher Q1 1998 company-wide revenue than Q1 1997 and 135,000 added registered customers since January 1. | Other customers and cohorts are outside the supplied transaction file; registered customers are not necessarily purchasing customers. Company growth does not prove the economics of this cohort or future cohorts. |
| Does company-wide growth establish that a cohort advance is safe? | CDnow reported a $9.2 million Q1 1998 net loss alongside $10 million of revenue. | A company net loss does not measure this cohort's contribution or cash available for repayments; cohort costs, cash timing, and collection terms are still needed. |
| Was there an industry-wide fall in demand for retail CDs? | Total recorded-music shipments fell in 1997, while full-length CD shipments to retail rose. | Manufacturer shipments and this cohort's online purchases are different measures. Neither identifies why individual customers returned or did not return. |

The supplied file intentionally selects people whose first-ever CDnow purchase was in Q1 1997; their presence in that quarter is guaranteed by the cohort definition. Compare like-aged activity and later-date repeat buying before interpreting the decline in calendar-quarter purchasing customers. CDnow acquisition spending by cohort and channel, variable costs, pricing, service experience, and comparable cohort histories are not in the file or these public reports. Do not use later market outcomes to explain or judge the July 1998 decision.

Do not use CDnow's full Q2 1998 company results unless their **publication date** can be verified as July 15, 1998 or earlier. A June 30 financial period end alone does not establish that investors could already read the results.
