## Claims removed or narrowed during review

- The January 19, 1998 archived CDnow homepage and the *San Francisco
  Chronicle* article could not be read through the available source tool in
  this audit. Their named-album prices and postage figures were removed from
  the tracked reports. The archive capture time alone would not prove the
  original page's publication date.
- The April 1 [*Wired* Lycos article](https://www.wired.com/1998/04/lycos-jumps-on-cdnow-deal/)
  has the generic attribution “Wired News Report.” Its cash/stock split and
  detailed placement description were removed. The attributed April 10
  article supports the total commitment used in the findings.
- The May 22 CNN Money/*Red Herring* MTV article was not readable in this
  audit, so its $22.5 million figure and editorial skepticism were removed.
  The retained Barnako item reports about $22 million. The amount difference
  does not change the cohort analysis and should not be silently resolved.
- The archived *Billboard* and *Radio & Records* PDF issues were not readable
  through the initial source tool. The March 21 *Billboard* issue was later
  inspected via its [Google Books page scans](https://books.google.com/books?id=2Q4EAAAAMBAJ&printsec=frontcover),
  with its byline and financial passages recorded in the
  [company evidence inventory](company_financial_evidence_1998.md). The
  *Radio & Records* issue remains unread; its 51% existing-customer sales
  share and advertising-spend figures were removed and must not enter the
  phase 3 forecast as verified facts.
- The *Quill* legal discussion was removed from the price comparison because
  it does not establish CDnow's actual checkout tax treatment. The open
  question remains whether the dataset's transaction dollars include item
  price, shipping, and tax.

Before the final investment memo, recheck the retained URLs and any new
external claims. If a primary CDnow filing or announcement dated by July 15,
1998 becomes available, prefer it for company figures and record any
difference from the third-party reports here.
